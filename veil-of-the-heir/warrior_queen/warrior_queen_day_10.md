I went to the Iron Garden at dawn. Habit. Nine days of pre-dawn training had carved the pattern into my nerves so deeply that the body rose, dressed, laced the corset — thirty-two minutes, a new record — and descended the stairs before the mind had finished deciding whether to participate.

The yard was empty.

No Aldric. No practice blade waiting on the rack. No Yseldra materialising from the flagstones with a bow and a stare that could freeze a river at midsummer. The cobblestones were clean, damp with morning dew, catching the first grey light in a thousand small mirrors. The training dummies stood at the far wall in their perpetual slouch, their straw guts spilling from wounds I had inflicted over nine days of drill. The weapons rack held its inventory — practice blades, the sand sack that Aldric had used on Day 2, leather shields, wooden dowels.

Silence. The Iron Garden, which had been the most densely populated twelve-hundred square feet of my life for the past week and a half, was deserted.

A note hung from the weapons rack. Parchment, pinned with a training nail. Aldric's brutal hand.

//Rest. One day. Your body requires it. The breast needs to heal. The muscles need to recover. The mind needs to process. Do not drill. Do not spar. Do not run. Rest.//.

//If I find you in this yard with a blade in your hand before tomorrow's dawn, I shall confiscate every weapon you own and replace them with a pillow.//.

//— A.//.

I read the note twice. The threat of the pillow was, I suspected, not entirely metaphorical.

I stood in the empty yard for several minutes. The silence was enormous. Without the clash of blades, without Aldric's clipped commands, without the rhythmic percussion of footwork on cobblestones, the Iron Garden was merely a courtyard — stone and air and the fading scent of sweat from a thousand years of warriors who had bled here and left nothing behind but the stains.

My hand drifted to the rapier at my hip. The fingers found the grip. The body //wanted// to draw — wanted the familiar weight, the balance, the conversation between hand and steel that had become the most reliable relationship in my life. Nine days of training had made the rapier an extension of the arm, and the arm without it felt //incomplete//. Amputated.

Aldric's note. The pillow.

I left the Iron Garden. The rapier stayed sheathed.

The bath was not scalding. For the first time in ten days, I chose the water's temperature — not Maera's prescription for bruised muscles, not the surgical heat of post-combat recovery, but a warmth that was simply //pleasant//. Warm enough to soothe. Cool enough to feel.

I sank into it with a groan that was less exhaustion and more //relief//. The relief of a body being asked nothing. No stances to hold. No blades to catch. No arrows to loose. No goblins to kill. The water held me and the water asked nothing in return, and the transaction was so simple, so generous, so unlike every other interaction my body had been subjected to this week that tears pricked my eyes for the stupidest possible reason.

Kindness. The water was being //kind// to me. And the body, which had been tested and compressed and bruised and struck and pushed to its limits for nine consecutive days, was so grateful for the kindness that it wept.

I let the tears come. Let them track down cheeks that were softer now — the cheekbones higher, the jaw less angular, the skin itself finer-grained. The tears fell into the bathwater and dissolved, and I floated, and I wept, and I let the body feel what it needed to feel without asking it to feel something more productive.

When the weeping passed, I looked down.

The transformation had accelerated. Whether from the potions' continued work or the physical demands of training — Thalara had warned that exertion could hasten certain changes, that the body under stress drew more heavily on the alchemical transformation, as though the effort of retraining catalysed the reshaping — the body in the water was //measurably// more feminine than it had been ten days ago.

The breasts had grown. Still small — still developing, still in the early stages of what Thalara's projections suggested would be a substantial eventual development. But //rounder//. Heavier. More //defined//. No longer the ambiguous buds of Day 1, the swells that could be mistaken for overdeveloped pectorals by anyone not looking too closely. These were //breasts//. Small breasts. Young breasts. But unmistakable in their shape, their weight, their relationship with gravity, their //presence// upon a chest that had been flat a month ago.

They floated in the warm water — two pale mounds breaking the surface, the nipples just above the waterline. The nipples were larger than they had been. The nubs thicker, more prominent, rising from areolae that had widened to the size of copper coins and darkened from pink toward a richer rose. The Montgomery glands — those tiny raised bumps that Veletia counted and Maera traced — were more numerous, more pronounced, dotting the areolae like braille that the body was writing in its own language.

The left breast bore the shadow of yesterday's bruise — faded dramatically by the moonpetal, reduced from spectacular purple to a faint yellow-green that would be invisible by nightfall. The tissue was still slightly swollen — the breast marginally larger than the right — but the inflammation was resolving, the flesh returning to its normal asymmetry.

Below the breasts, the waist had narrowed. The corset's daily compression was training the body — or perhaps the body was training itself, the transformation and the corset collaborating on a project that neither could achieve alone. The waist-to-hip ratio was changing visibly. The midsection that had been the prince's straight, columnar trunk was becoming a //curve// — inward at the waist, outward at the hips, the silhouette of a woman beginning to emerge from the chrysalis of the prince.

The hips were wider. The bones had migrated — a slow, grinding process that Thalara said would continue for months, the pelvis tilting and spreading to accommodate a body that was being rebuilt for a different mechanical purpose. The thighs were thicker. The muscle still present — strong, capable, the thighs that had powered the drop-step and the serpent-strike — but overlaid with a layer of softer flesh. Female fat distribution, depositing reserves in the places a woman's body stored them.

I lifted my hands from the water and examined them. Longer fingers. Narrower palms. The rapier calluses still present on the right hand — new calluses, building over the fading calluses from the longsword, two generations of warrior's marks in the same palm. The skin was softer. The knuckles less prominent. The nails — I noticed with a start — were growing differently. More oval. Less square. The cuticles smoother.

These hands had killed a goblin yesterday. These hands had held a blade to Cedric's throat. These hands had trembled in the moonlit yard and invented a technique from the back of an oak. These hands were becoming a woman's hands, and a woman's hands had done all of those things.

After the bath, I stood naked before the full-length mirror in my chamber.

I forced myself to look. Truly look. Not the hurried glances I had been stealing — the averted eyes and turned shoulders, the cowardice of a mind that did not wish to see what the body was becoming. Ten days ago, this mirror had shown me a prince whose body was beginning to betray him. Today —

The figure in the mirror was //slender//. Not the lean, hard slenderness of the prince — that angular, blade-edged leanness that had been carved from broad shoulders and flat planes. This was something softer. More //articulated//. The muscles were present but refined — visible beneath skin that was smoother, beneath a layer of flesh that padded the hard edges into curves. The body of a warrior who was also becoming a woman. A woman who was also becoming a warrior.

The breasts sat upon the chest like assertions. //This is real,// they said. //We are here. We are growing. Accommodate us.// Small still — barely filling my cupped hands — but shaped with a certainty that the buds of ten days ago had not possessed. The inframammary folds were deepening into proper creases. The undersides of the mounds were beginning to curve //under// — the first suggestion of hang, of weight responding to gravity independently of the ribcage. The nipples pointed slightly outward, the breasts' growth angling the tissue toward the sides in the pattern that Thalara's anatomy texts called //lateral migration// and that I called //going their own way//.

The waist curved inward. The navel sat at the centre of a stomach that was softer than it had been — the abdominal ridges that the prince had maintained through years of training blurring into a gentler plane. The core was still strong — the corset training and the feminine guard had built muscles that the old body never had — but the strength was //internal// now. Beneath a surface that was smooth and yielding.

The hips curved outward. The widening was visible even from the front — the flare from waist to hip described a ratio that was approaching, with each passing day, the geometry of a woman's body. The thighs touched where they had not touched before — the inner thighs meeting in the warm, soft contact that the prince had never experienced.

Between the legs — I looked. For the first time, I //looked//. Not long. Not with the detailed assessment I had brought to the breasts and the hips. But I //looked//, and what I saw was — changing. Slower than the rest. But changing. The transformation there was following its own timeline, and I acknowledged it the way I acknowledged the bruise on my breast — it existed, it was part of the process, and it would be dealt with when the time came.

The face. I met my own eyes in the mirror. The face looking back was not the prince's face. Cheekbones higher. Jaw softer. Lips fuller. Eyes larger in sockets that seemed to have widened slightly, the brow bone retreating, the features migrating toward a beauty I did not yet know how to wear.

The hair fell around the face in dark waves — past the shoulders now, nearly to the middle of the back, thick and lustrous with whatever alchemy Thalara's potions worked upon it. It framed the new face with a softness that the prince's cropped cut had never provided.

I stood. Looked. Let the mirror show me everything. The warrior and the woman. The blade and the curve. The calluses and the softness.

And I was done hating it.

Not done fearing it. Fear would be a companion for some time yet. But //hatred// — the visceral, scalding hatred of a body that was betraying its owner — that I released. It fell from me like the prince's old armour, piece by piece, clattering to the floor of a chamber that was too small for it and always had been.

This was the body I would wear to the throne. This was the body I would train into a weapon. This body — unfinished, imperfect, caught between what was and what would be — this body had killed a goblin and earned a village's kneel and put a blade to a knight's throat and invented a combat technique in a forest. This body had //proved itself//.

I pressed my hands against the breasts. Both hands, both mounds. The flesh was warm beneath my palms. The nipples pressed against the centres of my hands, two points of sensitivity that transmitted the contact inward, downward, through channels that the prince's body had never possessed.

<<speech "mc">>I see you.<</speech>>

[[Evening — solo drill by torchlight|warrior_queen_day_10_6]]

Aldric's note had said //do not drill//. The note had threatened me with a pillow.

I went to the Iron Garden anyway.

Not to fight. Not to spar. Not to hold stances or practice footwork or execute the three-guard sequence until my arms shook. I went because the yard was //mine// now — mine in a way that the prince's training ground had never been. The prince had used the Iron Garden as a tool. The person I was becoming used it as a //home//. The cobblestones knew my feet. The walls knew my shadow. The air knew the sound of my rapier.

I drew the blade. Slowly. Not to drill — to //hold//. To stand in the torchlit yard with the steel in my hand and the body in the between-space and the night turning overhead, and simply //exist//.

The rapier caught the torchlight and gleamed. Slender as a reed. Light as a whisper. The weapon that had killed a goblin and earned a village's kneel and taught my hand a new language.

I held it upright before me. The blade vertical, the guard at my chin, the formal salute. Not to an opponent. To the yard. To the training. To the ten days that had broken me apart and built me into something that could stand naked before a mirror and say //I see you//.

I sheathed the rapier. Stood in the torchlight. The breasts rested against my chest beneath the corset — small, growing, warm with the moonpetal's magic and the transformation's quiet work. The hips held my weight. The core held my centre. The breath lived in the belly.

I drank Thalara's potion in my chamber before sleep. The liquid was warm going down — not the bitterness of the daily formulations but something //sweeter//. Gentler. As though the alchemy had decided, tonight, to be kind.

The dreams came. Vivid, as promised. The completed body. The full breasts and the wide hips and the woman's face and the rapier that sang. In the dream, I fought — beautiful, fluid, lethal, a warrior queen in the Iron Garden she had earned through blood and sweat and the stubborn refusal to yield.

And in the dream, for the first time, I did not wake wanting to return to the prince.

I woke wanting to arrive at the queen.
