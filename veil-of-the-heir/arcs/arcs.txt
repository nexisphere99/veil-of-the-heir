## 👗 THE WARDROBE ARC — Full 50-Day Breakdown

### **The Wardrobe Arc: Silk and Steel**
*Mistress Veletia's Complete Feminine Attire Training*

**Arc Placement:** Runs parallel to combat training. Each "day" represents a wardrobe session — they don't need to be consecutive calendar days. Veletia teaches Caelan to dress, move, and present as a woman through clothing.

**Arc Theme:** *"Clothing is the visible spell of identity. What you wear declares what you are."*

---

**PHASE 1: FOUNDATIONS (Days 1-10)**
*"What touches the skin first shapes everything above it"*

**Day 1 — The Chemise**
First women's undergarment. A simple linen chemise — the layer worn directly against the skin beneath everything else. The sensation of fabric designed for a woman's body: longer, wider at the hip, shaped at the chest. The way it drapes. The way it moves against the nipples. The realization that even the most basic garment is gendered, and this one is female.

**Day 2 — Smallclothes**
Women's underwear. Not the loose linen braies the prince wore — shaped, fitted, designed for a body with wider hips, a rounder backside, and nothing significant between the legs to accommodate. The first time wearing fabric that is *cut for female anatomy*. The tightness at the hip. The smoothness at the front where no pouch exists. The awareness of what is and is not there.

**Day 3 — The Training Corset Revisited**
Veletia's combat corset vs. a daily-wear corset. Different construction — less boning, more flexibility, designed for long hours rather than violent motion. Learning to lace it alone. The morning ritual of wrapping the body in structure. The way posture changes. The way the waist looks.

**Day 4 — The Breast Band**
Precursor to a proper brassiere — a structured band with cups designed purely for support, without the corset's torso coverage. Lighter. Cooler. For casual wear, for sleeping, for the days between training. The first garment whose sole purpose is the breasts. The intimacy of wearing something designed to hold a part of the body that didn't exist a month ago.

**Day 5 — Stockings and Garters**
The sensation of silk or fine wool against newly smooth, nearly hairless legs. The garter belt — the small, fiddly, deeply *female* engineering of keeping stockings up. The way the legs look — longer, smoother, shaped by the fabric. The vulnerability of bare thighs above the stocking top.

**Day 6 — The First Casual Dress**
Not a gown — a simple day dress. The moment the trousers come off and the skirt goes on. The sensation of *air* against bare legs beneath fabric. The way a skirt moves — swinging, brushing, following rather than containing. Learning to walk in a skirt without tangling. Learning to sit without exposing. Learning to *be* in a garment that moves differently than anything the prince ever wore.

**Day 7 — The Sleeping Gown**
The first proper nightgown. Silk or fine linen, flowing, cut to the mid-calf. The experience of going to bed in women's clothing. The way silk drapes over the breasts at rest. The way the hem rides up during sleep. The intimacy of night-wear — the most private garment, worn only for the self.

**Day 8 — Shoes**
Women's shoes. Not boots — shoes. Lower-heeled, softer-soled, shaped for a foot that is narrowing. The way the gait changes with different footwear. The first pair of heeled slippers — even a low heel alters the posture, tips the pelvis, lifts the calves, changes the walk. The click of a heel on stone rather than the thud of a boot.

**Day 9 — Hair Accessories**
Pins, ties, ribbons, circlets. Veletia teaches braiding styles — the warrior's knot, the court braid, the loose waves for evening. The first decorative hair piece — a silver clip, small, functional, and yet unmistakably *ornamental*. The prince never wore ornaments. The queen will.

**Day 10 — The Full Foundation Look**
Everything together. Chemise, smallclothes, breast band, stockings, day dress, shoes, hair styled. The full image in the mirror. Veletia's assessment. The first time Caelan sees a *complete* feminine outfit on the body from head to toe and has to reconcile the reflection with the identity.

---

**PHASE 2: THE COURT WARDROBE (Days 11-25)**
*"In court, clothing is armour, and every seam is a strategy"*

**Day 11 — The Bodice**
The structured upper garment of court fashion. Boned, shaped, fitted to the bust and waist. The first neckline that shows the upper curve of the breast — cleavage, or the beginning of it. The vulnerability. The power. Veletia: "The bodice frames the breast the way a setting frames a gemstone."

**Day 12 — The Full Skirt**
Court skirts — heavier, wider, layered with petticoats. Learning to navigate doorways, sit in chairs, curtsey. The weight on the hips. The swish. The way fabric amplifies the body's shape.

**Day 13 — The First Complete Gown**
Bodice and skirt combined. Full court attire. The mirror moment. Caelan in a gown for the first time. The reaction — from Caelan, from Maera, from Veletia, from whoever else is present.

**Day 14 — The Formal Neckline**
Different necklines and what they reveal/conceal. The square neck, the sweetheart, the off-shoulder. Each one changes the body's presentation. The off-shoulder exposes the collarbone and the upper chest — the narrower shoulders, the delicate bone structure. The sweetheart frames the cleavage.

**Day 15 — Corsetry for Court**
The difference between the training corset and the court corset. More restriction. More shaping. The waist cinched smaller. The breasts lifted higher. The sensation of being *sculpted* by clothing. The discomfort. The result.

**Day 16 — Colors and Fabrics**
Veletia's lesson on what colours suit the changing skin tone, hair, and eyes. The prince wore dark, muted tones. The queen can wear anything — and Veletia has *opinions*. The first bold colour — emerald green, sapphire blue, deep wine red. The way colour changes how the body reads.

**Day 17 — The Riding Habit**
Women's riding attire. A fitted jacket, a split skirt or breeches cut for female hips, a tailored coat. The saddle-breast problem addressed through design. Elegant and functional. The first time Caelan rides in women's clothing.

**Day 18 — Formal Gloves**
Women's gloves — elbow length, fitted, a second skin over hands that are becoming smaller and more delicate. The intimacy of being gloved — the fingers enclosed, the touch filtered through silk or leather. The ritual of removing a glove — the slow peel, the reveal of bare skin, the gesture that is inherently *female*.

**Day 19 — Jewelry**
The first jewelry. Not the heavy signet ring of the prince — delicate, feminine pieces. A thin chain at the throat. Small earrings (if ears are pierced or magically prepared). A bracelet on the thinning wrist. The weight of adornment. The way it catches light and draws the eye to specific body parts.

**Day 20 — The Evening Gown**
Formal, floor-length, designed to be seen by candlelight. Darker fabrics, richer textures. A deeper neckline. More shaping. The gown that says: this is a woman who commands attention after dark.

**Day 21-23 — Walking, Sitting, Moving in Court Dress**
Three days of pure movement training in full gown. Veletia and Serelyne collaborate. How to walk in heels and skirts. How to sit without crushing fabric. How to curtsey (not bow). How to descend stairs without dying. How to dance in a gown — the way the fabric moves, the way partners interact with the skirt's volume.

**Day 24 — The Public Debut**
First appearance at court in full female attire. The Great Hall. Every eye. The walk from the entrance to the throne dais. The reaction — silence, whispers, and then... applause. Or not. The body on display, fully female, fully dressed, fully *seen*.

**Day 25 — The Day After**
Processing the debut. Gossip reports. Jeral's intelligence on who said what. The emotional aftermath. The pride, the exhaustion, the strange relief of having been seen and survived.
